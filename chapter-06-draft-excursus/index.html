    Chapter 6 Excursus — The One Handle of Covenant Hearing: Hypostatic Attitude (Expectation to Hear and Resolve to Obey) Ordering the Triadic Image (Nous–Logos–Pneuma)

    Purpose

    Chapter 6 argues that God’s speech is covenant-making nearness—and that sinners instinctively manage holiness by distance (Exod 20:18–21). This excursus supplies one practical “handle” by which covenant hearing becomes inhabitable and durable, using the book’s christological-anthropological grammar of person (hypostasis) and nature (physis).

    Bridge to the “Two Handles” language (Chapter 4)

    Chapter 4 named two “handles” of participation: (1) nature-side union (the Spirit’s gift enacted through Word–Table–Prayer), and (2) person-side watchful attention (hypostatic consent that inhabits the gift). Chapter 6 focuses on covenant hearing. At this point the two handles can be stated more simply as one practical handle with two aspects: the hypostasis expects to be addressed and resolves to obey—so the Spirit’s gift (nature-side) and the person’s consent (person-side) remain inseparable.

    By attitude we do not mean temperament or religious mood. We mean a hypostatic posture: the act of the who (the person) turning toward God in trust and consenting to be governed by God’s address. This person-side posture does not replace grace; it receives grace by directing the nature’s faculties toward their true object.

    In this book’s terms, the relevant “nature-side” structure is the triadic image—nous–logos–pneuma—not a psychology of parts. The Spirit renews the human being by re-ordering this triad toward God through union with the glorified human nature of Christ; the hypostasis learns to inhabit and persevere in that gift through a stable posture.

    This one handle has two inseparable aspects:

    1) Expectation to hear from God — the hypostasis chooses nearness and presents the nous for illumination and understanding.
    2) Resolve to obey — the hypostasis gives covenant “Amen,” so that the logos (confession) and pneuma (movement of love) take durable, embodied shape.

    Claim: Covenant hearing becomes durable when the person (hypostasis) holds expectation and resolve together, thereby allowing the Spirit to re-order the triadic image (nous–logos–pneuma) into communion-shaped obedience within the Church’s worship.

    Biblical Positive Models (three scenes of hypostatic nearness)

    Scripture does not only warn against distance; it also gives thick, positive models where persons and communities take up a posture of nearness and consent—and thereby become a people able to hear and obey.

    • Psalm 27:8 names the inner structure: God addresses (“Seek my face”), and the heart answers (“Your face, LORD, I seek”). This is hypostatic expectation responding to divine summons.
    • 2 Chronicles 34–35 (Josiah) shows the ecclesial enactment: the Book is found and read; the king and people respond with repentance and covenant renewal; reforms follow; and the movement culminates in Passover—a table of covenant communion restored.
    • Nehemiah 8–10 displays corporate consent: the people gather “as one,” ask for the Book, attend to the reading, receive understanding, repent and rejoice, confess sin, bind themselves in written covenant, and enact concrete reforms.

    Across these scenes, one pattern recurs: Word received as address → covenant “Amen” → life re-ordered → worship restored and strengthened.

    Terminology (person-side posture; nature-side triadic image)

    • Hypostasis (person): the acting subject—the “who” who turns, consents, refuses distance, and perseveres.
    • Nature (physis): the “what” that has real faculties and movements, healed and strengthened by grace.
    • Triadic image (nature-side):
    • Nous: heart’s sight / receptive perception (the faculty that can be darkened or illumined).
    • Logos: truthful confession / speech shaped by God’s Word (the “Amen” given form).
    • Pneuma (spirit): the movement of love/communion (the human being’s Godward and neighborward animation).
    • Attitude (hypostatic posture): the person-side stance of nearness and consent by which the hypostasis directs the triadic image toward God’s address.
    • Expectation (person-side act): the hypostasis seeking God’s face and presenting the nous to be taught—without demanding novelty or a particular feeling.
    • Resolve (person-side act): the hypostasis consenting to be governed—so that the logos and pneuma take concrete, obedient shape.

    Note on desire and practice: In patristic terms, “desire” and “habit” are not a replacement for the triadic image; they are energies and embodied “tropos” (mode) that follow from how the nous–logos–pneuma are ordered. When the triad is healed, desire is redirected and practice is reshaped.

    I. Aspect 1: Expectation — Hypostatic seeking that gathers the Nous (Ps 27:8; Neh 8)

    1) Expectation is a person-side turn toward God’s address

    Psalm 27:8 gives the structure: God speaks first—“Seek my face”—and the heart answers—“Your face, LORD, I seek.” Expectation is the hypostasis turning toward God: not curiosity, but consent to nearness.

    This is precisely what Exodus 20 lacks: Israel hears the voice and steps backward. Expectation is the contrary movement: stepping forward in trust that God’s nearness, though holy, is for life.

    2) Expectation presents the Nous for illumination (Neh 8)

    Nehemiah 8 shows expectation as a communal discipline:

    • the people gather “as one” (Neh 8:1),
    • they ask for the Book (8:1),
    • they attend (8:3),
    • they receive understanding through explanation (8:7–8).

    That is, the hypostasis chooses to attend and remain near, and the nous—the nature-side faculty of receptive perception—is gathered for illumination. Expectation is the hypostatic act that refuses dispersion and places the nous under God’s address.

    Here the Fathers are simply realistic. Origen expects Christ to speak in the Scriptures and therefore trains the “inner ear” by patient, ecclesial listening—so the same text becomes living address rather than dead record.

    3) Expectation aims at understanding that strengthens the whole person

    Nehemiah 8 moves from understanding to stability: “the joy of the LORD is your strength” (Neh 8:10). The point is not emotional uplift; it is covenant endurance. When the nous receives God’s Word as address, the human being is strengthened to live the covenant rather than retreat from it.

    Expectation is the hypostasis choosing nearness—seeking God’s face—so the nous can be gathered and illumined for understanding that strengthens covenant life.
    Before → After (Frame Shift):
    Before: hearing from afar (information without encounter)
    After: the person draws near so the nous can truly receive God’s address.

    II. Aspect 2: Resolve — Hypostatic “Amen” giving Logos and Pneuma durable shape (2 Chron 34–35; Neh 9–10)

    1) Resolve is covenant consent, not moral self-manufacture (Josiah)

    When Josiah hears the Book, he receives it as divine claim, and his response takes covenant form: “to walk after the LORD… and to keep his commandments… with all his heart and all his soul” (2 Chron 34:31).

    This is resolve in hypostatic terms: the person consents to be governed. And because it is covenant consent, it is allegiance within God’s mercy—obedience as the fruit of renewed communion.

    2) Resolve gives Logos a public form and Pneuma a communal direction (Neh 9–10)

    Nehemiah 9–10 shows resolve moving from moment to durable, communal shape:

    • confession (Neh 9) tells the truth about God and about us,
    • covenant (Neh 10) gives the community’s “Amen” a public, accountable form,
    • reforms enact love of God and neighbor in concrete practices.

    In triadic terms: resolve shapes logos (truthful confession and vowed fidelity) and directs pneuma (the movement of love/communion) into concrete obedience. This is why Scripture insists that hearing must become doing.

    Augustine helps name what is at stake: the Word does not merely inform; it re-orders love. Resolve is not bare exertion; it is the hypostasis consenting to a new order of desire and communion.

    3) Resolve culminates in restored worship and feast (2 Chron 35)

    Josiah’s reform flows into Passover (2 Chron 35). The sequence matters: hearing leads to covenant consent, which leads to a restored table of communion. Resolve is ordered toward worship and love, not grim performance.

    Chrysostom, preaching Hebrews, warns that repeated hearing without response hardens the heart. Resolve is the refusal to let “Today” pass without obedient consequence.

    Resolve is the hypostasis giving covenant “Amen,” so that logos (confession) and pneuma (love/communion) take durable, embodied shape in obedient practices.
    Before → After (Frame Shift):
    Before: “Amen” as mere assent and obedience as vague intention
    After: hypostatic consent that forms truthful confession and concrete love.

    III. The Synergy: One handle, two aspects (person-side acts opening the triadic image to healing)

    Expectation and resolve must remain together, or “attitude” collapses.

    • Expectation without resolve becomes spiritual consumption: the nous may be stirred by hearing, but the hypostasis refuses the concrete obedience that would give logos and pneuma durable form.
    • Resolve without expectation becomes moralism or externalism: the hypostasis tries to force obedience while keeping God’s address at a functional distance; the nous is not gathered, and the heart hardens.

    The positive biblical scenes bind the two:

    • In Nehemiah, attentiveness (expectation) leads to confession and covenant (resolve).
    • In Josiah, hearing leads to covenant consent and reform, and culminates in Passover communion.
    • In Psalm 27, seeking God’s face implies both receptive nearness and a life reordered around that nearness.

    In short: expectation presents the nous for illumination; resolve gives logos and pneuma concrete shape. Both are hypostatic acts by which the person inhabits the Spirit’s gift.

    Attitude is one handle because covenant hearing is one movement: expectation and resolve together allow the Spirit to re-order nous–logos–pneuma toward communion-shaped obedience.
    Before → After (Frame Shift):
    Before: either “insight without obedience” or “effort without address”
    After: the person receives God’s address and answers with obedient Amen.

    IV. Practical Rule: A hypostatic posture that trains Nous–Logos–Pneuma toward obedience

    Because attitude is a person-side posture, it is trained by repeated actions—chiefly in worship and then carried into the “liturgy after the liturgy.”

    A simple weekly + daily rule:

    1) Seek (before worship): pray Ps 27:8—“Your face, LORD, I seek”—as the hypostasis refusing distance.
    2) Attend (during the Word): keep the person’s gaze on God’s address. Receive one phrase as command or promise; let it gather the nous.
    3) Amen (in creed/prayers): speak the Church’s “yes” as covenant consent; let it give logos a truthful, public form.
    4) Do one act of love/obedience (after worship): choose one concrete act that matches the Word (reconciliation, restitution, mercy, truth-telling, a kept promise). This is not legalism; it is the safeguard against self-deception (Jas 1:22–25), and it gives pneuma concrete direction.
    5) Renew midweek: reread a short passage, pray one sentence, and complete the same obedience—so the “Amen” does not evaporate and the heart does not drift back to distance.

    This rule does not manufacture grace. It simply keeps the hypostasis near enough to be addressed and steady enough to obey—so the Spirit can re-order nous–logos–pneuma toward covenant life.

    The handle becomes practical when worship trains expectation and one concrete obedience stabilizes the Church’s Amen—so nous–logos–pneuma are reordered toward love and fidelity.
    Before → After (Frame Shift):
    Before: worship as attendance and evaluation
    After: worship as hypostatic consent that yields embodied obedience.

    Frame Shift (Excursus-level)

    Before: I keep God’s Word manageable—content to assess or rules to perform—while standing at a safe distance.
    After: I draw near expecting God’s address, and I answer with covenant “Amen” that becomes obedience—so the Spirit re-orders nous–logos–pneuma toward communion-shaped life.
