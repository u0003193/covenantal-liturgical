    Covenantal–Liturgical Christian Life

    The Christian life as participation in God’s covenantal presence through Christ

    What This Book Is

    This book argues that the Christian life is covenantal and liturgical before it is moral or psychological. Scripture presents the life of faith not as a technique for inner experience nor as a program of ethical self‑improvement, but as participation in God’s covenantal presence, fulfilled in Christ and enacted in the Church’s worship.

    From Sinai to Zion, from Temple to Christ, from Pentecost to the New Jerusalem, the Bible narrates a single Covenant–Temple drama: God forms a people by speaking, dwells with them in mercy, and draws them into communion so that they may live as a royal priesthood. This book traces that drama and shows how it shapes Christian formation, worship, ethics, and daily life.

    Central Claim

    The Christian life is the Spirit‑empowered participation in God’s covenantal presence through Christ, enacted as New Covenant liturgy—the once‑for‑all offering of Christ made present for communion in the Church’s ordo of Word, Table, and Prayer—and extended into daily life as living sacrifice, the offering of our bodies in covenantal love.

    Method and Sources (Variant A)

    This book proceeds canonically and theologically, not reconstructively. Scripture is read as a unified drama ordered toward Christ, with particular attention to Temple, covenant, sacrifice, and divine presence as Scripture’s own pedagogical grammar.

    The argument is developed: - Canonically: tracing patterns from Eden to the New Jerusalem - Christologically: reading all Scripture from the Paschal center - Ecclesially: locating participation within the Church’s worship - Patristically: drawing on the early Church Fathers as disciplined readers of Scripture within liturgy - Formationally: showing how worship trains perception (nous) and issues in embodied love

    Patristic witnesses (e.g., Irenaeus, Origen, Athanasius, Basil, Cyril of Jerusalem, Chrysostom, Augustine) are used not as parallel authorities but as guides who preserve Scripture’s participatory logic—guarding against both reductionist symbolism and ritual mechanism.

    Modern scholarship and ressourcement theology are engaged selectively and subordinated to the biblical‑patristic synthesis.

    How to Read This Book

    This is not a collection of standalone essays. The argument is cumulative.

    • The Introduction names the problem and stakes: what it means to live the Christian life once access to God has been opened “once for all.”
    • The Prologue supplies the governing lenses for the entire book, especially the Throne–Ladder / Name / Entry framework.
    • The Parts unfold the Covenant–Temple drama step by step: Israel as pedagogy, Christ as fulfillment, the Church as participation, and the Body as the arena of daily obedience.
    • The Coda gathers the argument eschatologically, moving from hearing to seeing in the New Jerusalem.

    Readers may consult individual chapters, but the book is best read in order.

    What This Book Is Not

    • Not a manual for spiritual techniques
    • Not a reduction of Christianity to ethics or interior experience
    • Not a denial of Christ’s once‑for‑all sacrifice
    • Not a replacement of Scripture with liturgy or tradition

    Rather, it argues that Scripture itself teaches us to live liturgically, because God forms His people by Word, sacrament, and Spirit toward love.

    Intended Readers

    This book is written for: - pastors and teachers - theologians and advanced students - serious Christian readers seeking a Scripture‑shaped account of formation

    It assumes careful reading but not specialist training.
